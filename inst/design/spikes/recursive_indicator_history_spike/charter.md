# Charter: Recursive-Indicator History Spike

**Status:** Draft charter, written by Claude at the maintainer's request on
2026-10-07 and rewritten after peer review on 2026-10-08; awaiting maintainer
acceptance. Binds nothing.
**Protocol:** `inst/design/spike_protocol.md`
**Probe:** `dev/spikes/recursive-indicator-history/probe_findings.md`
**Feeds:** the joint WS3/WS4 history-start decision that replaces Cut 9's
temporary restart rule, and the recursive-indicator RFC due in the pipeline
(its "failure-sensitive sampled prefix-invariance and fn/`series_fn`
diagnostics" scope in the roadmap's leakage row).

## 1. Question

> Can a mechanical check that uses only an indicator's own definition
> classify every indicator ledgr accepts as bounded, decaying or
> path-dependent, and flag the case where its `fn` and `series_fn` disagree,
> without false alarms on bounded indicators?

That is the only question. Its answer decides whether the history-start rule
can treat indicators by class (for example, hydrate a bounded indicator by
`stable_after` bars) without a new declaration, and whether the silent wrong
value of probe P3b can be caught. It designs neither the rule nor an API.

The check has two parts on sampled positions of a seeded series:
- **history sensitivity:** compute the series from two or more history starts
  and compare values on common days after warmup;
- **definition agreement:** compare `fn` on its declared window with
  `series_fn` at the same positions.

## 2. Cheaper Prerequisite

> Does the internal feature engine a dense run uses
> (`ledgr_compute_feature_series()`, not exported) reproduce exactly the
> values a public dense `ledgr_run()` sees, for every indicator in section 4?

If it does, the check can run on the engine directly and cheaply. If it does
not, the spike stops; a recharter would measure through public runs only.

## 3. Runnable Core

No package fork is needed. The core is one harness function,
`classify(indicator, bars)`, built from public indicator constructors and the
internal engine named above, returning the two parts of the check and a
class. The prerequisite is its first case.

## 4. Cases

Generated by running the core; expected answers are frozen only after it has
run, in git, with the reviewer named. One frozen classification per
indicator, plus the prerequisite, gives nine cases within the cap of ten:

- bounded: `ledgr_ind_sma(5)` (declares `strict_window`), `ledgr_ind_sma(20)`,
  `ledgr_ind_ema(5)`, and a custom rolling maximum whose `fn` and
  `series_fn` agree;
- decaying: TTR `EMA(5)` and TTR `RSI(14)` through `ledgr_ind_ttr()`;
- path-dependent: the probe's `running_max`, undeclared and declared
  `strict_window` (the P3b case).

Each runs on two seeded paths: the probe's sine-and-trend series and a seeded
random walk. Decay distances are not measured; for an EMA they follow from its
smoothing constant and add nothing a declaration needs.

## 5. Evidence

The runner writes one CSV row per indicator and path: the largest
history-sensitivity gap after warmup at the sampled positions, the largest
`fn` and `series_fn` gap, the tolerance used, the declared `requires_bars`,
`stable_after` and `gap_contract`, and the resulting class. Every row is
derived by computation over the seeded bars; none is narrated.

## 6. Kill And Recharter

Stop and return to the maintainer if any of these happens:

- the prerequisite fails (section 2);
- no single tolerance separates the bounded indicators (gaps near 1e-13)
  from the decaying ones on both paths, so the check needs per-indicator
  knowledge;
- a sampled check cannot tell decaying from path-dependent behavior on these
  paths, so classification needs a declaration after all;
- an indicator falls outside the three classes.

## 7. Deliverables And Gut

Exactly three executor deliverables, under
`dev/spikes/recursive-indicator-history/`:

- `spike_runner.R`, writing the classification CSV and the prerequisite row;
- `spike_checker.R`, rerunning into a scratch directory, diffing against the
  recorded CSVs and guarding package scope (`R`, `src`, `tests`, `NAMESPACE`,
  `DESCRIPTION`, `man`, `inst/design`);
- `spike_inventory.md`, naming what was demoted, deleted and learned.

The gut removes the definition-agreement part. The declared-`strict_window`
running max must then pass as an ordinary indicator on a named row, the P3b
defect going undetected, while every other classification is unchanged.
Merely changing bytes is insufficient.

The closeout is the protocol's one-page terminal record under
`inst/design/spikes/recursive_indicator_history_spike/closeout.md`.

## 8. Non-Goals, Open Scope And Budgets

No burn-in declaration field, hydration mechanism, warmup API, gap-transition
rule, availability-mode recursion, or fix for P3b is decided or written; the
spike only shows whether a check can see them. Its thresholds are fixture
facts, not product tolerances.

Open and owned elsewhere: Cut 9's LTB-0196 already pins run, sweep,
promotion, window and walk-forward parity for a recursive TTR EMA under the
restart rule; pulse inspection (`ledgr_pulse_snapshot()`) under the history
rule belongs to WS3. Gap transitions need availability-mode recursion, which
today is refused unless an indicator declares the strict window; they stay
with the recursive-indicator RFC.

Budgets: charter 150 lines; harness 1,500 R lines; single correction 500
lines; closeout one page. An overrun stops the spike.
