# Measuring backtest engines against point-in-time guarantees

A causal daily-equity backtest over a historical index universe is correct only if about fifty testable guarantees hold together. This report sets out 48 universal correctness guarantees and 24 optional capabilities, with a 41-scenario synthetic test catalogue that exercises every universal guarantee at least once. The guarantees with the largest documented effect on results are these: membership as of each session, not later membership; fills strictly after the information they use; prices as traded, with corporate actions applied as events; and terminal events settled at their real value, not the last print. The documented distortions include a sign reversal of the low-volatility effect on current S&P 500 constituents ([Deutsche Bank, practitioner](https://columbia.edu/~nyc2107/papers/seven_sins_of_quantitative_investing.pdf)), a fall in a reversal strategy's Sharpe ratio from 1.4 to 0.3 when same-close fills are removed (same source), and an average −30% value for performance delistings whose returns are missing from CRSP ([Shumway 1997](https://www.tylergshumway.org/Shumway-DelistingBiasCRSP-1997.pdf)). No vendor reviewed supplies announcement times or knowledge-time vintages for index membership. Sharadar documents its S&P 500 dates as effective dates ([Sharadar](https://sharadar.com/docs/sp500)), and Norgate does not document what its constituent flag means ([norgatedata](https://pypi.org/project/norgatedata/)). As a result, the adapter's sealed snapshot is the only available proxy for "what was known when", and the engine has to enforce visibility itself. The evidence is weakest exactly where an auditor would want numbers. There is no peer-reviewed survivorship figure for S&P 500-constituent backtests, no literature on state carried across walk-forward folds, and no study of which unknown-settlement policy minimises bias for large caps. The report marks these places rather than filling them in.

## How to read this standard

**Scope.** The standard covers daily US equities traded through historical index-membership universes, with S&P 500 since 1998 as the reference case. Notes on other markets and intraday data come at the end of Section A. It specifies what must be true of results. It does not cover how frameworks split ownership, and it does not evaluate any engine.

**Notation.** A session `s` is a trading day from the sealed calendar. `τ(s)` is the decision time for session `s`; for a close-based daily strategy this is the session close in ET (16:00, or 13:00 on early-close days). `avail(x)` is the earliest time datum `x` was publicly knowable. `member(i, s)` says whether instrument `i` belongs to the universe on session `s`.

**Classes.** **U** marks a universal correctness requirement: if it is violated, results are wrong, biased or unauditable. **C** marks an optional capability: without it a feature or strategy type is unavailable, but results that are produced are not wrong. Universal rows appear in sub-tables A.1–A.9 by area. All capability rows are collected separately in A.10, so the two kinds of gap never mix.

**Owners.** *Data* is the adapter that produces sealed inputs. *Engine* is the day-by-day runner. *Research* owns the strategy and the universe definition.

**Evidence tags.** Each cell in the failure-mode column is tagged [peer-reviewed], [primary] (regulator, exchange, index provider, vendor documentation or platform source code), [WP] (working paper), [practitioner], [preprint], [forum] or [inference]. [inference] marks engineering reasoning from the research notes that no source states directly. The guarantees themselves are a synthesis: they are what the cited evidence implies an engine must do, and they do not quote any published standard. No such published standard was found.

Labelling backtests as hypothetical and keeping them separate from live track records is a regulatory obligation for marketed results. It does not affect correctness. The SEC Marketing Rule treats backtests as hypothetical performance, the SEC fined nine advisers a combined $850,000 in 2023, and the archived GIPS Q&A prohibits linking backtests to actual performance ([SEC 2023-173](https://sec.gov/news/press-release/2023-173), [GIPS Q&A](https://www.gipsstandards.org/?p=5323)). SR 26-2 replaced SR 11-7 on 17 April 2026 and targets banks above $30bn in assets. Outcomes analysis and backtesting remain validation activities under it, but it does not prescribe reproducibility ([SR 26-2](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr2602.htm)).

### A.11 Where other markets or intraday data change a guarantee

Four things change outside daily US data. **Multi-market strategies** make time zones part of U5.2 and U6.3. A Tokyo close on day D is available before the New York open on D, but a New York close on D arrives only after Tokyo's D+1 decisions; QuantConnect cites differing close times as the reason it adopted precise bar end times ([QC 17380](https://www.quantconnect.com/announcements/17380/precise-market-open-and-close-times-for-daily-data/)). **Price-limit markets** make "limit-locked" a tradability state that U4.2 must refuse. In China, regular stocks have a 10% daily limit and special-treatment (ST) stocks 5%, and prices are drawn toward the limit ([VoxChina](https://voxchina.org/show-3-49.html)). Hong Kong's historically long suspensions would make stale-mark escalation (C4.2) material there, but no source was retrieved for that, so it is unverified. **Intraday engines** have to model LULD pauses and early-close auction cut-offs (C4.1, C5.2). Exchange calendars also treat lunch breaks as closed time ([exchange_calendars](https://github.com/gerrymanoim/exchange_calendars)). **Rules-based indices** such as Russell can be recomputed from rank-day data, whereas S&P membership can only be replayed. Russell's staged lists also need C1.3 if a strategy trades them.

| 12 | Readiness, gap policy, fold-boundary state, resumption drift | U7.1, U7.2, U8.4, U9.5 | Documented failure mechanisms only: QuantConnect's warm-up runs once ([QC forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/13019/explaining-iswarmingup-isready-setwarmup/)), and reduced-precision restarts diverge ([GROMACS](https://manual.gromacs.org/2023/user-guide/managing-simulations.html)). | No published magnitude. |

Ranks 1, 2 and 5 are also the defaults an unaudited stack is most likely to have. A universe built from a current list, close fills, and last-price exits on delisting are each the simplest implementation. LEAN and Zipline both use the last price on delisting ([LEAN source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QuantConnect/Lean/master/Engine/TransactionHandlers/BrokerageTransactionHandler.cs), [Zipline ledger](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/quantopian/zipline/master/zipline/finance/ledger.py)). Zipline fills at the close of the bar being simulated ([Zipline slippage](https://zipline.ml4trading.io/_modules/zipline/finance/slippage.html)).

## D. Open questions with competing positions

**How large is survivorship bias in S&P 500-constituent backtests?** There is no verified answer. Fund studies put it near 1% a year and growing with sample length ([Carhart et al.](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jcarpen0/pdfs/CarhartCarpenterLynchMusto.pdf)). Non-US constituent studies find 3–5 pp a year, but one of them reverses sign under value weighting ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19380)). The one S&P-specific demonstration is a practitioner chart ([DB](https://columbia.edu/~nyc2107/papers/seven_sins_of_quantitative_investing.pdf)). The standard should treat U1.4 as binary (complete or not) and should not cite a magnitude.

**Is the index effect gone, and does announcement timing still matter?** Greenwood & Sammon's abstract gives 0.3% for additions in the last decade, while their body gives 1.0%. S&P DJI's own study calls the effect "in a structural decline" ([G&S SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4294297), [Preston](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/research/article/what-happened-to-the-index-effect-a-look-at-three-decades-of-sp-500-adds-and-drops)). A 1998-start backtest still includes a decade in which the stakes ran to several percent per event, so U1.2 stays universal even if recent stakes are small.

**Effective or announced membership as the default, and what counts as knowledge time?** Index funds hold from the effective date, which makes effective membership the conservative default. Announcement data is needed for event strategies, but none of the vendors reviewed supplies it. Sharadar documents "the effective date of the change" without saying whether that means before the open or at the close ([Sharadar](https://sharadar.com/docs/sp500)). Norgate's constituent flag is undocumented on both points, so it has to be tested empirically against S&P press releases ([norgatedata](https://pypi.org/project/norgatedata/)).

**Does membership need full bitemporal storage?** The notes infer that membership corrections are transcription fixes rather than revisions of the world, so pinned vintages are enough (C1.2 rather than U). No source discusses this. Russell's staged preliminary lists are a real counter-case in which knowledge does evolve ([Russell 2026](https://lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/ftse-russell/2026/russell-reconstitution-2026-schedule)).

**Which policy should apply when settlement terms are unknown?** There are four candidates. Shumway's constants (−30% NYSE/AMEX, −55% Nasdaq) are grounded in OTC evidence but are averages over CRSP-wide samples ([Shumway](https://www.tylergshumway.org/Shumway-DelistingBiasCRSP-1997.pdf)). Hou, Xue & Zhang use same-type 60-month means and find little impact under value weighting ([HXZ](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394.pdf)). CRSP itself leaves the value missing, and CIZ does not impute ([SWW](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/26501_Rewriting_CRSP_History.pdf)). Platforms use the last price, which is the most optimistic option. No study tests these policies on large-cap index universes since 1998, which is why U2.5 requires a declared policy plus a sensitivity report rather than a specific number.

**Can same-close fills ever be legitimate, and how much reversal profit is real?** Conrad, Gultekin & Kaul attribute short-run contrarian profits to bounce ([CGK](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bes/jnlbes/v15y1997i3p379-86.html)). Lehmann (secondary) and de Groot et al. find net profits in large caps ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-short-term-return-reversal/), [de Groot et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i2p371-382.html)). The standard sidesteps the dispute: a same-close fill is legitimate only for signals computable before the auction cut-off (C6.1), which requires intraday data.

**Is back-adjustment a form of look-ahead?** The practitioner who documented SPY's shifting history concedes, "whether one considers this look-ahead bias to be a real issue or not" ([Portfolio Optimizer](https://portfoliooptimizer.io/blog/adjusted-prices-without-look-ahead-bias/)). Ratios between two adjusted prices are unaffected by later rescaling, but level rules are not ([DB](https://columbia.edu/~nyc2107/papers/seven_sins_of_quantitative_investing.pdf)). The standard therefore restricts adjusted data to as-of ratio use (U3.2) instead of banning it.

**When should dividend cash be credited?** Booking cash on the ex-date matches CRSP daily RET ([CRSP](https://www.crsp.org/wp-content/uploads/guides/CRSP_Calculations_and_Index_Methodologies.pdf)). Zipline books a receivable on the ex-date and pays cash on the pay date ([Zipline ledger](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/quantopian/zipline/master/zipline/finance/ledger.py)). Both produce correct NAV. They differ only in when cash can be used (C3.2).

**How should gaps inside an indicator window be handled?** Skip, fill and invalidate all have defenders, and no authoritative source prescribes one. The notes report this as a documented absence. The standard requires only that the choice be explicit and flag-driven (U7.2).

**Walk-forward or combinatorial purged cross-validation?** Walk-forward is the only scheme in which every out-of-sample decision is causal, but it evaluates a single path with high variance. CPCV yields a distribution of paths, but some of its models are fitted on later data, so it measures overfitting rather than simulating what a trader would have earned ([Purged CV summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation), [PBO](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/42)). The two answer different questions. The standard makes walk-forward's causality universal (U8.2) and CPCV a capability (C8.1). Hold-out alone is called "unreliable" by the PBO authors.

**What state should carry across walk-forward folds?** No academic, practitioner or regulatory source was found. U8.4 is engineering inference: both continuous and independent modes are coherent, and silently mixing them is not.

**How severe is false discovery in finance?** Harvey, Liu & Zhu say most claimed findings are likely false ([HLZ](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen find most factors replicate ([JKP](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432)), and Chen bounds the false discovery rate at 9–25% ([Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15365)). Much of the gap comes from treatment of microcaps, weighting, and what counts as "replicate". Everyone agrees the trial set must be recorded (U8.5).

**Bit-identical reproduction or reproduction within a tolerance?** Scientific codes achieve bit-identity only on a fixed platform ([GROMACS](https://manual.gromacs.org/2023/user-guide/managing-simulations.html)). Regulators require results to be supportable and explainable, not bit-identical ([SR 26-2](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602a1.pdf)). The standard's position, bit-identical on a fixed platform manifest and within a documented tolerance across platforms (C9.1), is inference. No finance source covers it.

**When should a stale mark be written down?** CRSP's ten-period window ([CRSP](https://www.crsp.org/wp-content/uploads/guides/CRSP_Calculations_and_Index_Methodologies.pdf)) is the only documented anchor. No backtest valuation standard from GIPS or elsewhere was found, so the escalation threshold stays a research choice (C4.2).
